The dominant story in product design for the last fifteen years has been "remove friction." And most of the time, that's right.

But some friction is load-bearing. A confirmation step before a destructive action. A pause before you publish. The small resistance that makes someone ask themselves if they really want to do the thing.

## Where friction earns its keep

- Stakes are high and irreversible
- The user is likely operating on autopilot
- The system can't infer intent confidently

## Where it doesn't

Everywhere else, honestly. Default to smooth, add friction with a reason.
