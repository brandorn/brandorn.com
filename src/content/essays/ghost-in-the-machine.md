There is a lot of writing about how to come up with good product ideas. There is also a lot of writing about how to ship software that works. There is comparatively little about what happens between those two things.

## The middle is where products die

In my experience, the bit between "we know what we want to build" and "it's in production" is where the majority of product work actually lives. It's also where most of the ambiguity sits, and most of the judgment calls get made.

## A few principles

1. **Scope is a shape, not a size.** The goal isn't to do less; it's to do the right less.
2. **Write it down before you design it.** If the prose version is confusing, the UI version will be worse.
3. **Leave seams visible where the feature is still young.** You'll want to move them later.
