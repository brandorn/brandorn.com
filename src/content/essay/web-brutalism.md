There's a thread running from mid-2010s web brutalism to the interfaces I find myself returning to day after day. It's not the aesthetic itself — the monospace fonts, the unstyled hyperlinks, the gray backgrounds — but the posture behind it. A willingness to let the machine show through.

## The seam, not the seamless

The dominant story of the last decade of design has been "seamless." Make the friction disappear. Smooth the edges. Bring the experience closer to something that feels inevitable, magical. Apple made a career of this.

But there's a counter-current, quieter but growing, that says: seams are information. Seams are honesty. Seams are where a user gets to actually *understand* what's happening.

### Notion as a case study

I keep coming back to Notion because Notion wears its primitives on its sleeve. Blocks. Databases. Pages that are also databases. The mental model is exposed, and it rewards the user who takes the time to learn it with compounding power.

## Where this lands

I think a lot about the interfaces I want to build. And more and more, I want to build interfaces that trust the person on the other side of the screen.

Not brutalism as a style. Brutalism as a *stance*.
